How this works

Sporeline keeps track of the pieces of your cultivation projects and how they descend from each other. Everything you log is one of four things.

Names

Two fields: strain is what you call it, species is the formal name behind it. The strain leads everywhere it is shown, with the species alongside; if you only know the species, that stands on its own.

Type a strain you have used before and the species fills itself in. Change it whenever the pairing is different.

IDs

Every entry gets a six character ID like MY2K7P. The first two letters are the kind, the next character is the generation, and the last three are random.

So MY2K7P reads as: mycelium, second generation, and K7P tells it apart from your other second generation plates. Generation 0 is anything you did not make yourself. Ten and beyond use a letter, so an ID is always six characters.

The random characters skip I, O and 0 — they are the ones you misread off a piece of tape six weeks later.

Family trees

Open any entry and you will see its family drawn out, oldest generation at the top. By default it shows that entry, its siblings and everything made from them. There is a link to widen it to the whole genetic family. Every box is clickable.

Gone, not deleted

Everything is either on hand or gone. When you use up a jar or toss a contaminated tub, mark it gone and say what happened — that reason shows as a small label on the entry. It stays in your log and in the family tree forever, just dimmed.

Recording the reason is worth the extra two seconds: after a season you can filter for everything lost to contamination and see whether it clusters on one genetic, one step, or one time of year. Deleting is only for entries you created by mistake.

Your data

Everything lives in a folder on this computer: {{.Data}}. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Sporeline also keeps dated copies of your log in a backups folder inside it, one a day, so a mistake is something you can undo. You can change that on the Settings page.
