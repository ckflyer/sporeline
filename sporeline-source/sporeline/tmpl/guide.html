How this works

Sporeline keeps track of the pieces of your cultivation projects and how they descend from each other. Everything you log is one of four things.

IDs

Every entry gets a six character ID like MY2K7P. The first two letters are the kind, the next character is the generation, and the last three are random.

So MY2K7P reads as: mycelium, second generation, and K7P tells it apart from your other second generation plates. Generation 0 is anything you did not make yourself. Ten and beyond use a letter, so an ID is always six characters.

The random characters skip I, O and 0 — they are the ones you misread off a piece of tape six weeks later.

Family trees

Open any entry and you will see its family drawn out, oldest generation at the top. By default it shows that entry, its siblings and everything made from them. There is a link to widen it to the whole genetic family. Every box is clickable.

Gone, not deleted

When you use up a jar or toss a contaminated tub, mark it gone. It stays in your log and in the family tree forever, just dimmed. Deleting is only for entries you created by mistake.

Your data

Everything lives in a folder on this computer: {{.Data}}. Nothing is sent anywhere. Back that folder up, or use the button on the Data page.

Credit where it is due

Sporeline follows the design of mycolog by Richard Ulmer, which is MIT licensed. The four kinds of component, keeping things you no longer own instead of deleting them, the family tree rules, and the label alphabet that skips I, O and 0 are all his ideas.

Sporeline is a separate program rather than a fork — none of his code is in it — but it would not exist without mycolog, and if you want a smaller tool backed by SQLite, use his.
